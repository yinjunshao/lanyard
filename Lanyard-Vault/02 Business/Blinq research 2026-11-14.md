# Blinq research (2026-11-14)

- Blinq is a large, well-funded digital business card and lead capture platform (Australian company, US-heavy customers). It sells one-way sharing of your details and, for paying teams, badge scanning with CRM sync. It is not a two-way "how we met" memory.
- It is free for individuals (2 cards) and charges teams per card; event lead capture is a paid Business feature priced per lead. Nothing found aimed at students or UK societies.
- Lanyard is stronger on two-way context (shared selfie, note, "Come to me for"), host-pays-attendee-free pricing, and event hosting for small events. Blinq is stronger on reach, sharing channels, wallet and NFC, CRM sync and AI enrichment.
- Best ideas to borrow without breaking Lanyard's rules: a post-event follow-up nudge, a Wallet pass for the badge, "save to phone contacts" with the context, and event-over-event reports for hosts. Details in section 10.
- All figures read on 2026-11-14. Blinq's own site and independent sources are separated below. Some pages (G2, Capterra) refused to load directly (403), so their numbers come from search snippets.

## 1. What it is and who it is for
**Blinq's own site says:** an "AI-powered contacts platform" for first impressions: digital business cards, contact capture, remembering who you met, and follow-up. It targets individuals (it names networkers, realtors and small business owners), teams and businesses, enterprises, and event organisers/exhibitors. It claims users at about 93% of the Fortune 500.

**Independent:** TechCrunch (May 2025) describes it as business-to-consumer-to-business: people adopt it personally, then companies pay per seat. About 80% of customers were in the US then. It names Mobilo, Popl, Wave and Wix, plus LinkedIn and Linktree, as competitors. A third-party review site (dupple, which carries affiliate links and does not describe hands-on testing) says it suits field sales teams and conference-heavy organisations. No page found targets students, universities or societies.

## 2. Features
Tags: [site] means Blinq's own pages; [indep] means an independent source also says it; [site only] means not independently confirmed.

**Cards and design**
- Multiple cards for different purposes, more than 20 fields, custom links, colours and fonts, logo in the QR code, Blinq branding removable on paid plans. [site] [indep: app store listing via mwm.ai]
- Card counts: 2 free, up to 5 on Premium. [site]

**Sharing**
- QR code, link, text and email, NFC tap (physical card sold separately), Apple and Google Wallet pass, iPhone NameDrop and App Clip, home-screen widget, Apple Watch, email signature, video-call virtual background. [site] [indep: app store listing, dupple]
- The recipient opens the card in a browser and does not need the app or an account. [site, support centre] Some reviewers on Capterra and Google Play still complain that non-users are pushed to download the app (see section 6).

**Exchange and lead capture**
- "Share back": the other person can send their details to you; they get a temporary card with a QR code for 3 days, emailed a copy, and can claim it with a free account. No paid plan is needed on either side. [site, support centre]
- Universal scanner for badges, paper business cards, QR codes and LinkedIn profiles; works offline and syncs later. [site]
- Event lead capture (Business plan and above): one campaign per event, custom lead forms and qualifiers (for example hot/warm/cold), lead capture mode for unstaffed booths, cost-per-lead and rep reports, attribution by event. Can pull registration data where an organiser opens a badge API (Cvent is named). [site] Claims of 5 seconds from scan to CRM and 90%+ enrichment coverage are vendor claims, not verified.
- AI enrichment: fills in email, phone, title, LinkedIn from a name and company. Pro and above. [site only]
- AI notetaker: records and transcribes conversations (phone, Bluetooth mic or watch) and summarises them; prompts the user to ask permission. Free plan keeps 30 days of notes. [site] [indep: app store listing]

**Integrations**
- Native CRM: Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Pipedrive, Zoho, GoHighLevel, Marketo, monday.com. Outlook contacts. SSO and provisioning via Okta, Entra ID and Google Workspace. Zapier. Calendar booking via Chili Piper. An MCP connection so Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot (Grok listed as coming) can query your contacts. [site] [indep: dupple confirms five native CRMs, the site lists more]

**Teams and admin (Business and Enterprise)**
- Team cards and templates, brand control, admin-managed email signatures, automated card provisioning, company directory, admin dashboard, individual and team analytics, SSO, SCIM, SOC 2 Type II (report on request). [site]

**Not confirmed:** any specific "events" product for attendees to meet each other (no matchmaking, rooms or event hub found); a student or education offer (none found in search); analytics detail for individuals.

- **Free:** 2 cards, unlimited sharing (QR, widget, email, SMS, wallet), unlimited contacts and tags, universal scanner, email signature, virtual backgrounds, AI notetaker limited to the last 30 days.
- **Premium:** up to 5 cards, custom colours and design, logo on the QR, branding removed.

- **Business:** team cards, brand control, directory, event lead capture with badge scanning, campaigns, native CRM, admin dashboard, analytics. Lead capture is $9.99 per lead captured and enriched on the monthly view, billed monthly (a separate lead-capture page says pay-as-you-go for 1 to 3 events a year, custom packages for 4 or more). Duplicates are not charged.
- **Enterprise:** subteam admins, enforced SSO, SCIM, invoice billing, customer success manager.
- **NFC cards** (Blinq blog, via search result): standard $24.99, wood $29.99, metal $49.99, custom $59.99 to $79.99; 10% to 25% bulk discounts from 20 cards. An older page showed $19.99, so this changes.
- Independent pages disagree with each other and with the pricing page (a Blinq comparison blog says the free plan has a scanner capped at 5 scans a month; dupple lists other numbers). Re-check before quoting.

## 5. Privacy and data
Blinq's privacy policy (fetched 2026-11-14; the page showed an update date of 23 October 2026, though a search snippet showed 21 May 2026, so check the date) says:
- Entity: Blinq Technologies Pty Ltd, Australia. It cites Australian law, GDPR and UK GDPR, and US state laws. It names a data protection officer contact through a third-party service.
- It does not sell personal information. It shares with service providers (hosting, cloud, AI transcription, payments, analytics such as Google Analytics, Segment, Amplitude, Datadog; Twilio for calls; printers and shippers for physical goods), affiliates and authorities where required.
- It collects data about non-users: names, work contact details, profile photos and public professional information, sometimes added by users or enriched from third-party data providers. Users are expected to have a basis for adding others.
- Recordings: users must get consent from everyone recorded. Call audio is deleted once transcribed; in-person audio is kept 90 days on free accounts and for the life of the subscription on paid ones. It says audio, transcripts and summaries are not used to train AI and its AI providers are barred from doing so.
- Legal bases: consent, contract, legal obligation, legitimate interests. Rights to access, correct, delete, object, port and withdraw consent.
- Storage: may be processed outside Australia, the EU and the UK, including the US, Canada, Germany, Ireland, Belgium and Finland; Standard Contractual Clauses for EEA and UK transfers. No single data location is stated.
- Blinq's site claims SOC 2 Type II and GDPR and CCPA compliance. Independent audit of these: Not confirmed.

## 6. What users say
Sources: Capterra, G2, Trustpilot and app-store figures reached only through search snippets or aggregator pages; the review sites themselves returned 403 to direct fetches.
- **Praise:** quick setup; a free tier that is enough for many people; easy sharing by QR; good templates, colours and fonts; handy when out of paper cards.
- **Complaints:** price, with some reviewers saying the paid tiers or price rises are too steep for a card; saving and sharing bugs (a card sometimes sends the recipient to a search page instead of saving); scanning problems; limited editing and account sharing on lower tiers; recipients sometimes told to download the app; slow loading; some CRMs not syncing smoothly; contacts not always landing in the phone's contact list instantly.
- **Ratings (reported, not independently audited):** about 4.9 on the iOS App Store (about 160,000 ratings per mwm.ai), about 4.8 on Google Play, about 4.8 on G2 (about 9,100 reviews), about 4.8 on Trustpilot (about 1,030 reviews), about 700 reviews on Capterra. Blinq's own page claims 4.9 over 200,000 reviews; this is a vendor total and the sum of the above is lower.

## 7. Company facts
- Founded 2017 as a hobby project in Melbourne, Australia; founder and CEO Jarrod Webb. (TechCrunch, 6 May 2025.)
- US$25 million Series A, May 2025, led by Touring Capital, with Blackbird Ventures and Square Peg Capital returning and HubSpot Ventures new. (TechCrunch; Blinq's own blog.)
- Team grew from 5 to 67 people; offices in Melbourne, Sydney, San Francisco and New York. (TechCrunch, Blinq blog.)
- Users: about 2.5 million people and 500,000 companies in 189 countries in May 2025 (Blinq blog and TechCrunch); the home page now claims about 4 million people (site only, Not confirmed). More than 7,500 businesses manage cards through it (Blinq blog, May 2025).
- Valuation reports (about AU$200 million) came up in search results from business press; not checked against a primary source, so Not confirmed.

## 9. Where each is stronger
**Lanyard is stronger on**
- The memory of how you met: selfie, note, what they can help with, event context, all with both people's consent. Blinq mostly moves your details to someone else.
- Fit for its audience: free for students, society-friendly host pricing, UK-first, no named institution, no install.
- Event-wide features Blinq does not offer attendees: check-in, the room, who to meet, drops, feedback survey, host present page.
- Privacy by design: no enrichment from data brokers, no recording of third parties, hosts see aggregates unless an event turns on named attendance.
- Pricing shape: a host pays for an event rather than a per-lead fee that grows with every contact.

**Blinq is stronger on**
- Scale, funding and trust: millions of users, a US$25m round, SOC 2 Type II (vendor claim), 4.8 to 4.9 ratings reported.
- Distribution points: Wallet pass, NFC, email signature, virtual backgrounds, watch, widgets, App Clip, NameDrop.
- Capture breadth: badge, paper card and QR scanning, offline capture, enrichment.
- Business plumbing: CRM sync (8 native), SSO and SCIM, admin dashboard, team templates, campaign reporting.
- AI: enrichment, a conversation notetaker, MCP access from outside assistants.

## 10. Feature ideas for Lanyard, ranked
Fit key: **Fits** means it keeps the rules (free for attendees, hosts pay, no app download, privacy first, no hardware or NFC per D013, UK student first). Nothing here has been added to the Master To-Do or plan notes; the owner chooses. Checked against F01 to F34: none of these is built.

| Rank | Idea | Why it matters for Lanyard | Size | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | After-event follow-up nudge: one email (or in-app prompt) a day after an event, "You met N people, add a note or draft a follow-up", to the person only | Blinq's pitch is "follow up while context is fresh"; retention between events is Lanyard's biggest risk ([[Business Model]]) | S to M | Fits. Email to self only; hosts see nothing; respect the mailing list rules in F26 |
| 2 | Apple and Google Wallet pass for the badge QR | Puts the QR on the lock screen at a fair; Blinq lists this as standard; already named "not built" in [[Features]] | M | Fits (no app). Check pass signing costs and Apple developer account |
| 3 | Save a connection to phone contacts (vCard) with name, photo and "how we met" in the notes field | Gives the connection a home outside Lanyard; Blinq's contacts sync is a repeated user complaint when it fails | S | Fits. Only for the person's own connections; selfie stays in Lanyard unless they choose |
| 4 | Host reports across events: compare this term's events on connections per attendee, return rate, feedback | Blinq's campaign attribution is what buyers pay for; careers services need termly proof for [[Customers and Buyers]] | M | Fits. Aggregates only |
| 5 | Sponsor or exhibitor booth QR with attendee opt-in lead sharing | Blinq's paid use case is exhibitors; [[Business Model]] has sponsor leads as the likely biggest later revenue | L | Fits only if each person opts in per sponsor, with a clear notice; extends D038 thinking, needs a lawyer view |
| 6 | Share card in a CV or email signature: a printable "my Lanyard" QR and link snippet | Students put links on CVs and applications; low cost | S | Fits. Does not need NFC |
| 7 | Host-branded card templates for society committees or company staff | Mirrors Blinq team templates; lets a society give its committee a matching look | M | Fits. Hosts pay; attendees unaffected |
| 8 | Webhook recipes and a Zapier-style or CSV export of aggregate and opted-in lead data for CRMs | Careers and company hosts may use a CRM; Lanyard has only a generic webhook | S to M | Fits for aggregates; named data only where F31 or opt-in applies. Low value for societies |
| 9 | Dictate a private note by voice (device speech-to-text on the person's own note) | Faster notes at a noisy fair; Blinq's notetaker draws interest | S | Fits only if it records nothing of the other person and stores text only. A recording notetaker conflicts (below) |
| 10 | "Book a coffee chat" link from a connection (the person's own booking link) | Blinq links Chili Piper booking to a lead; helps with follow-up | S | Fits. Low priority for students |

**Ideas that conflict with Lanyard's rules**
- **NFC cards or tap-to-share:** conflicts with D013 (no hardware or NFC for now). Revisit only if D013 changes.
- **Recording conversations with an AI notetaker:** conflicts with privacy first and consent: it captures the other person's voice and words. Blinq's own policy puts the consent duty on the user.
- **Contact enrichment from third-party data providers:** conflicts with privacy first and aggregate-only data; it pulls details about people who never agreed to be in Lanyard.
- **Scanning paper cards or badges of people who are not on Lanyard:** conflicts with the two-way, consent-first model and stores non-users' data; low value for students who rarely carry paper cards.
- **Per-lead pricing for hosts:** conflicts with D001 (attendees free, hosts pay a flat event fee) and would reward harvesting contacts.
- **Native apps for iOS or Android:** conflicts with no download.
- **Enterprise CRM-first features (Dynamics, Marketo, SCIM):** poor fit for UK student buyers; consider only for a licensed university or company customer.
