# Go-to-Market

## Beachhead: UK university events
UCL access is direct and free; pricing is in pounds. China is a later, localised play (WeChat dominates). See [[D006 UK campus beachhead first]].

## Stages
**1. Free pilots (next 1-3 months):** 5-10 events (society socials, hackathons, careers events). Measure sign-up rate, connections per attendee, selfie share, 7-day return. Collect a quote from every host.
**2. First paid:** charge a small flat fee for the next event; goal is one paying customer. A "no" at £15-25 is more informative than ten "sounds interesting".
**3. Sponsors and institutions:** sponsor packages inside events; termly plan for careers services using pilot data.

## Validation before more building
Interview 10-20 organisers; 3-5 free pilots in return for "official networking tool" placement, a testimonial and permission to publish aggregate numbers. See [[D007 Validate before building]].

## Growth loops
- "Made with Lanyard" on every scanned card; one-tap make-your-own.
- Society sees Lanyard at another society's event; free first events remove the objection.
- Referral credit: a host who refers another gets a free event, so does the new one.
- Campus ambassadors: one student per society.
- "Powered by Lanyard" on free events; paid hosts can hide it.

## Access routes (this is where help is needed)
Introductions to organisers, entry to events, and people with resources. See [[Network and Introductions]].

## Pitch to hosts (10 slides)
Hook ("You met 30 people last night. How many names do you remember?") → host problem → attendee problem → solution → live demo → host features → sponsor features → traction → pricing → ask ("Run Lanyard at your next event, free").
