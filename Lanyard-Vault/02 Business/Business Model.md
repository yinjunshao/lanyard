# Business Model

## Shape: B2B2C
Attendees use Lanyard free and create the network. Organisers pay, because they are measured on networking and sell sponsorship on it. See [[D001 Hosts pay, attendees free]].

| Layer | Customer | Pays for | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free personal | Students, attendees | Nothing | Now |
| Host events | Societies, institutions, companies | Check-in, live room, report, branding | First revenue |
| Careers services / unions | University staff | Termly plan, cohort reports | After pilots |
| Sponsors / employers | Recruiters | Branding, booth QR, opt-in leads | Likely largest, later |
| Plus (personal) | Heavy users | More cards, custom strap | Low priority |
| Enterprise / licences | Universities, big organisers | Annual licence, SSO, DPA | Year 2+ |

## Why events solve cold start
A networking tool is useless at density one. A host who says "this is how we connect tonight" gives 50-300 users at once. See [[Go-to-Market]].

## Principle: break even on societies, earn higher up
Society events cost almost nothing to serve, so price near cost and let sponsors, institutions and organisers fund growth. See [[D008 Societies near cost, profit higher up]].

## Honest assessment (from the plan docs)
- Societies have small budgets; charge sponsors, not societies.
- Careers services are a realistic buyer but slow; start with a free pilot.
- Professional organisers pay if it replaces badges or an event app; win on light, cheap, no download.
- Retention between events is the biggest risk. See [[Risks]].
- Any revenue projections in earlier docs (e.g. CNY ARR figures) are invented, not evidence. Decide from pilot data. See [[Metrics and Kill Criteria]].
